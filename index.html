Land, settle, eat in Ikebukuro

Arrival day
  • Kagurazaka神楽坂
  • Ikebukuro池袋

What to expect

You land at Haneda around 08:50 after the overnight flight, and the apartment check-in time is the open question, so this day stays short and close to base. Kagurazaka is a hilly old geisha quarter of stone lanes, small French bistros and bakeries, right on your doorstep. Ikebukuro is the opposite: a huge station city of department stores, anime shops and food halls, loud and bright, and the right place for a big evening meal.

  • Drop bags, walk the Kagurazaka lanes at your own speed, early lunch nearby.
  • Take the Yamanote line or Metro to Ikebukuro for dinner. Rikimaru is all you can eat, so pick unagi or ramen instead if you land tired.
  • Back to bed early.

Day 2Fri 6 Nov

Mitsui Outlet Park Kisarazu

Fixed
  • Mitsui Outlet Park三井アウトレットパーク

What to expect

A full day at a large open-air outlet by Tokyo Bay: roughly 300 shops across Japanese and international labels, with a big food court and a seaside feel. Weekdays are quieter than weekends there. Plan four to five hours of shopping, and eat inside the park. The food court and restaurants stay open after the shops close.

  • Take the direct weekday highway bus from Tokyo Station Yaesu Bus Terminal B12. The 09:05 bus arrives at Mitsui Outlet Park at 09:55, just before the 10:00 opening. Fare is ¥1,500 one way; journey is about 50 minutes.
  • Weekday Tokyo departures run frequently from 08:45 through 12:25, so 09:05 is a good target without arriving excessively early.
  • Return buses from Mitsui Outlet Park to Tokyo Yaesu run from 14:30 through 20:30. Useful options are 16:30 → 17:20, 17:30 → 18:20, 17:55 → 18:45, or later if you want a longer shopping day. Confirm the return schedule at the outlet's bus terminal on arrival — it's the only way back and times were not confirmed at time of writing.
  • Shops 10:00 to 20:00. Food court 10:30 to 21:00. Restaurants 11:00 to 21:00.
  • Buy the bus ticket before boarding and check the operating schedule on the day in case of service changes, especially if the Aqua-Line is affected.

What to expect

Three stops on one train line, each with a different mood. Kichijoji has Inokashira Park, lakeside paths and a tangle of small shops and snack counters, and it is busy on Saturdays. Koenji is vintage clothing, live music houses and neighborhood izakaya, relaxed and a little scruffy. Nakano has the Broadway arcade of collectibles and anime goods.

  • 11:15 Hikiniku to Come Kichijoji (booked).
  • Walk it off at Inokashira Park, grab a menchi katsu or yakimusubi to snack on.
  • Chuo line to Koenji for vintage shops, then Nakano.
  • Chuo line runs straight on to Shinjuku if you want dinner there.

What to expect

A slower day. The center of gravity is Nihonbashi and Ginza; Kiyosumi-Shirakawa is a genuine optional add-on, not a required start. Nihonbashi is the old merchant district, all department stores and long-running shops. On Sundays Ginza's main avenue closes to traffic from noon, so the stroll is the point, with plenty of choice for dinner.

  • If you're up early and motivated, start at Kiyosumi-Shirakawa for coffee and MOT, then continue to Nihonbashi. If not, go straight to Nihonbashi — that's the day's real start.
  • From 12:00 walk Chuo-dori in Ginza. The pedestrian zone runs to 17:00 in October to March, weather permitting.
  • Dinner in Ginza. Tsukiji pins are a short walk away if you want seafood.

Tokyo DisneySea

Fixed
  • Tokyo DisneySea東京ディズニーシー

Strategy & priorities

Prioritize what is unique to DisneySea rather than repeating experiences you already know from Disneyland Paris and Shanghai. Use Premier Access selectively if a priority attraction develops a long wait; do not optimize for maximum ride count. The tier list below is ordered by priority: S first, then A, then B.

Practical

  • Park hours: 09:00 to 21:00. Tuesday is a weekday.
  • Route: Take the Tozai Line from Kagurazaka directly to Kasai Station — no subway transfers. At Kasai, go to Platform 10 for the SS07 or SS08 bus. Both go to Tokyo DisneySea (SS07 stops at Disneyland first). Take whichever arrives first. Bus ride is about 19–30 minutes; pay when you board, around ¥320.
  • Arriving by 8:45: The Tozai Line is very crowded between 7:30 and 9:00. Leave Kagurazaka before 7:00 to avoid the worst of the rush and still arrive with time to spare.
  • Alternative: Tokyo Station → Keiyo Line → Maihama. The train is often less crowded than the metro, but the transfer walk inside Tokyo Station is long (about 15 minutes) and the Keiyo Line has its own 8:00–9:00 peak. Only worth it if you strongly prefer a seat on the train over a shorter total trip.
  • Table service reservations open at 10:00 Japan time one month ahead, which is Sat 10 Oct, 08:00 in Jakarta.
  • Fantasy Springs restaurants take mobile orders only.
  • Same-day re-entry is allowed with a handstamp and your ticket. Ikspiari at Maihama Station is about 20–25 minutes away (beyond the 500m rule). It's an alternative only if you want a cheaper, more filling meal outside the park — expect 15–20 min via monorail or 20–25 min on foot each way. Options include Tonkatsu Wako, Kua'Aina, Tsurutontan. Otherwise, stick to the in-park options above.

Park first, then fashion

What to expect

Start calm and end composed. Yoyogi Park and the cafes beside it are quiet in the morning. Harajuku is youth fashion on Takeshita Street and quieter side lanes behind it. Omotesando is the tree-lined avenue of flagship stores and architecture, and Aoyama beyond it is back-street galleries and small cafes. Mukai, a seafood bowl house in Tomigaya beside Yoyogi Park, makes a good lunch stop.

  • Morning at Yoyogi Park with coffee, then Fuglen. Both are in Tomigaya, a few minutes from Mukai.
  • Lunch at Mukai (open 10:45 to 14:30) or in Harajuku, then walk Omotesando into Aoyama.
  • Shibuya is one stop away if you want to extend into the evening.
  • Pack tonight. You leave around 06:00–06:30 for Haneda tomorrow morning.
